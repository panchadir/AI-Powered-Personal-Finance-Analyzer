# Rohan — The Money-Managing Partner

> 🚀 SECONDARY PERSONA

**Document:** Trigger Map — Secondary Persona
**Created:** 2026-07-07
**Priority:** SECONDARY TARGET
**Role in Flywheel:** The household multiplier — turns one calm user into a calm couple, and unlocks the Premium tier

---

## Profile Summary

Rohan is 35, Priya's husband, an IT project lead earning around ₹95,000 a month. In their household he is the *de facto* CFO — not because he's better with money, but because someone had to be, and he volunteered by default. He runs the family's finances out of a mix of duty and low-grade anxiety, juggling two incomes, shared EMIs, a joint rent, and the slow-building question of a home-loan down payment. He is the reason the Premium "couples" tier exists: he wants a *shared* view of "are we okay?", not two separate apps telling two separate stories.

He is more financially confident than Priya, but that confidence is brittle — it's held together by mental math and a running total in his head that no longer fits. What he wants is not to be taught; it's to be *relieved of the load* of being the household's single point of financial failure.

---

## Background

Rohan has always been "the responsible one." He started tracking the family budget in a spreadsheet years ago, kept it religiously for a while, and gradually let it rot as life got busier and the number of moving parts grew. Now money lives in his head: which EMI hits on which date, roughly how much is in which account, what's coming up. It works until it doesn't — until a bill he forgot collides with a month he misjudged, and he's the one who has to explain it.

Two incomes should feel like *more* security. Instead they feel like more *coordination* — two salary dates, two sets of spending, shared goals with no shared dashboard. He and Priya have the "are we okay this month?" conversation more often than either would like, and it usually happens at the worst time: when something has already gone wrong.

---

## Current Situation

Rohan's anxiety is different from Priya's. Hers is *avoidance*; his is **vigilance fatigue**. He looks too much, not too little — constantly holding a fragile running total, cross-checking two accounts, and carrying the mental weight of being the one who'd be blamed if the household missed something. His three moments:

- **BEHIND:** *Did we, as a couple, stay on track?* — reconstructing a shared month from two people's scattered spending.
- **NOW:** *Can we safely spend on this together?* — a weekend plan, a gift, a small upgrade, needing a joint answer.
- **AHEAD:** *Are we on pace for the big goal?* — the home-loan down payment, the thing all the vigilance is secretly for.

**How he solves this today:** a decaying spreadsheet, mental math, and periodic stressful "state of the union" money talks with Priya. He is the single point of failure, and he feels it. Multi-bank reality bites him hardest — a commitment paid from his account, a balance checked in hers, no unified truth.

---

## Psychological Profile

Rohan's core need is **shared load, not more control.** He already has (the illusion of) control; what he lacks is *relief* and a *second set of trustworthy eyes*. The product wins him by becoming the neutral, always-on household CFO that removes the "it's all on me" weight — and, crucially, by giving him and Priya **one shared number to trust** instead of two arguable ones.

Because he's more numerate, he is also more skeptical. He will *test* the Safe-to-Spend number against his own mental math, and the moment it's confidently wrong, he's gone. Honesty-under-uncertainty matters to him for a slightly different reason than Priya: not to feel safe, but to feel the tool is *rigorous* enough to hand the load to. He evaluates trust the same Indian-consumer way — credentials → security → social proof → features — but weights *security* and *rigor* highest.

## Driving Forces

### 💚 Positive Drivers (What Rohan wants)

**Want 1 — One shared, trustworthy "are we okay?" number**
He wants a single household truth he and Priya both see, instead of two private running totals.
> **Product Promise:** The Premium **Shared Confidence Score** and a household Safe-to-Spend give the couple one agreed number, updated for both — ending the "wait, which account?" confusion and the recurring state-of-the-union arguments.

**Want 2 — To be relieved of being the single point of failure**
He wants the mental load of "if I forget, we're in trouble" to be carried by something reliable.
> **Product Promise:** The **Financial Guardian** proactively ring-fences EMIs and committed obligations and speaks first when something's off — so the household's safety no longer depends on Rohan remembering everything.

**Want 3 — Visible pace toward the big shared goal**
He wants to know they're genuinely on track for the home-loan down payment, not just guessing.
> **Product Promise:** The **Financial Timeline** projects the couple's forward path and shows progress toward named goals — turning a vague hope into a visible, on-pace-or-not trajectory.

### 🛑 Negative Drivers (What Rohan wants to avoid)

**Fear 1 — Being blamed for a household money mistake**
His dread is a missed EMI or a blown budget landing on him because he was the one "in charge."
> **Product Answer:** Conservative-by-default Safe-to-Spend + ring-fenced commitments mean the tool actively prevents the missed-obligation scenario — and because it's shared, responsibility stops being his alone.

**Fear 2 — Endless coordination overhead**
He's tired of reconciling two accounts and two people's spending by hand.
> **Product Answer:** **Multi-account aggregation** (Paid/Premium) unifies the household's accounts into one view with visible freshness/confidence indicators — so multi-bank reality is handled honestly, not left as manual homework.

**Fear 3 — Trusting a number that turns out wrong**
As the skeptical, numerate partner, he fears handing off control to a tool that's confidently inaccurate.
> **Product Answer:** Every number carries **Confidence %** and explicit reasoning; under uncertainty the AI gets *more* conservative and transparent, and freshness/latency is shown, never hidden. Rigor he can audit is exactly what lets him finally hand off the load.

## Transformation (Summary)

😤 **BEFORE:** Rohan carries the household's finances in his head, cross-checks two accounts, and braces for the next "are we okay?" talk — security through exhausting vigilance.
😌 **AFTER:** He and Priya share one honest number and a visible path to their down payment; the Guardian watches the EMIs so he doesn't have to. He's relieved of being the single point of failure — and he's the one who upgraded them to Premium.

---

## Role in Flywheel

Rohan is the **household multiplier**. He does two strategic jobs:

1. **He doubles the value of one Priya.** A calm individual is good; a calm *couple* is stickier, harder to churn, and worth more — he is the reason the Premium tier converts. One acquired user becomes a household.
2. **He is a high-credibility referral node.** As the numerate "responsible one" among his peers, when Rohan vouches that a tool is *rigorous and honest*, other money-managing partners listen. He extends the advocacy engine into a more skeptical, higher-ARPU audience.

He does **not** get more design attention than Priya — the product is built for her first, and Rohan is served by *extending* her experience to a shared household context, not by a separate product.
