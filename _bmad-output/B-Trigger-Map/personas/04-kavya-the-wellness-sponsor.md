# Kavya — The Workplace Wellness Sponsor

> 🌟 TERTIARY PERSONA (Decision-Maker / Distribution Channel)

**Document:** Trigger Map — Tertiary Persona
**Created:** 2026-07-07
**Priority:** TERTIARY TARGET (Year-2 B2B2C amplifier)
**Role in Flywheel:** The distribution multiplier — buys calm for hundreds of Priyas at once

---

## Profile Summary

Kavya is 38, Head of People & Culture at a 1,200-person IT services company in Bengaluru. She is not an end user of the copilot — she is a **decision-maker** who could put it in the hands of hundreds of Priyas at once. Her currency is retention, productivity, and demonstrable employee wellbeing, and she is under quiet pressure to show that the money she spends on "wellness" actually moves those numbers rather than just looking good in a deck.

She matters to this product not in Phase 1, but as the **Year-2 amplifier**: the channel that turns proven consumer PMF into scaled, sponsored distribution. She is included in the trigger map deliberately — the B2B2C incentive is real and quantified — but with an equally deliberate guardrail: *chasing her too early would dilute the Priya focus that everything depends on.*

---

## Background

Kavya has bought wellness programs before — meditation apps, gym reimbursements, an EAP hotline — and watched most of them post single-digit engagement and vanish at renewal. She's become allergic to "wellbeing theater": benefits that sound caring but produce no measurable change. She has learned that the only wellness spend that survives budget scrutiny is the kind she can tie to a hard business metric.

She has also noticed something her leadership hasn't fully connected yet: her most anxious, most financially-stretched employees are also her flight risks and her quiet under-performers. She doesn't have data to prove it — but the pattern is there, and research backs her instinct (financial anxiety correlates with ~40% higher job-search likelihood).

---

## Current Situation

Kavya's decision-making runs on three questions:

- **BEHIND:** *Did the last wellness spend actually do anything?* — usually, embarrassingly little.
- **NOW:** *Can I justify a new line item to Finance?* — only if it comes with a measurable outcome.
- **AHEAD:** *Will this reduce regretted attrition and the cost of replacing good people?* — the number her CFO actually cares about.

**How she solves this today:** she buys generic wellness benefits, hopes for engagement, and struggles to prove ROI at renewal. Financial stress specifically is a blind spot — she has no tool that addresses it *and* reports back a defensible outcome.

---

## Psychological Profile

Kavya's core need is **defensible, measurable outcomes.** She is empathetic about employee stress, but she survives professionally on proof. She will not be won by the emotional pitch that wins Priya; she is won by *aggregate, privacy-safe evidence* that anxiety dropped and engagement was real — plus airtight assurance that she is not exposing her company to a data or regulatory liability.

Her trust order inverts the consumer one: for her, **credentials and security come first and loudest.** Before she cares that employees love it, she needs to know the product is a compliant FIU, DPDP-ready, that it never gives "financial advice" (SEBI boundary), and that it will *never* expose an individual employee's finances to the employer. One whiff of "are you selling my employees' stress data?" and the deal is dead — the exact industry trust failure the product is built to answer.

---

## Driving Forces

### 💚 Positive Drivers (What Kavya wants)

**Want 1 — A wellness benefit that produces a measurable outcome**
She wants proof, not participation trophies — something she can defend at renewal.
> **Product Promise:** Aggregate, anonymized cohort reporting on anxiety reduction and engagement gives her a defensible, board-ready outcome — the rare wellness spend tied to a hard number.

**Want 2 — A lever on regretted attrition**
She wants to reduce the loss of good people, and financial stress is an untapped lever.
> **Product Promise:** By measurably reducing employee financial anxiety — the factor linked to ~40% higher job-search likelihood — the copilot targets a genuine, quantified retention driver, not a vague "wellbeing" halo.

**Want 3 — High employee uptake with zero rollout friction**
She wants a benefit people actually use, without an IT project or an app-adoption battle.
> **Product Promise:** WhatsApp-first, zero-install delivery means sponsored employees start getting value from day one — no app store, no onboarding project, no low-end-device excuses. Uptake is the whole point, and the channel is built for it.

### 🛑 Negative Drivers (What Kavya wants to avoid)

**Fear 1 — Buying another piece of "wellbeing theater"**
Her dread is spending budget on something with 4% engagement that dies at renewal.
> **Product Answer:** The consumer product is engineered for daily habit (≥70% briefing open rate) *before* it's ever offered to her — she's buying a proven-sticky product, not a hopeful pilot.

**Fear 2 — Data / regulatory liability**
She fears exposing employee financial data, or a compliance breach landing on her desk.
> **Product Answer:** Regulated **FIU** posture, **DPDP Rule-4-ready** consent architecture, strict **individual-data-never-shared-with-employer** separation, and an explicit "here's what we do NOT share" stance — credentials and security lead the pitch, in her exact trust order.

**Fear 3 — Crossing into "financial advice" and its liability**
She fears sponsoring a tool that gives employees regulated financial advice her company is now on the hook for.
> **Product Answer:** All output is framed as **"information the user's own data shows," never advice** (SEBI Investment Adviser boundary), backed by a formal legal opinion — keeping both the product and the sponsor safely outside advisory liability.

## Transformation (Summary)

😒 **BEFORE:** Kavya buys wellness benefits that post single-digit engagement and can't be defended at renewal, while her most financially-stressed people quietly become her flight risks.
😏 **AFTER:** She sponsors a benefit employees genuinely use daily, gets privacy-safe proof that anxiety dropped, and can point Finance at a real retention lever — wellness spend that finally survives scrutiny.

---

## Role in Flywheel

Kavya is the **distribution multiplier**, and her timing is the strategic point:

- She converts *proven* consumer PMF into **scaled, sponsored acquisition** — buying calm for hundreds of Priyas in a single contract, at near-zero per-user CAC.
- She only works *because* the engine works: she buys the anxiety-reduction outcome that Priya's cohort already proved. Without a validated Priya, there is nothing to sell her.
- **Guardrail (load-bearing):** She is explicitly a **Year-2** target. Pursuing her in Phase 1 would pull design and roadmap away from Priya and dilute the single focus everything depends on. The brief is emphatic: do not dilute Phase 1. Kavya is the amplifier you switch on *after* the engine is proven, not the engine itself.
